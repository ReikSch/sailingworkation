06 · FAQ

Remote work,
just with more sea.

How reliable is the internet?

Starlink is a core part of the Workation concept and is very reliable in practice – comparable to a good connection on land. We still cannot guarantee 100% availability aboard a boat, for example in case of technical issues, exceptional weather or regional restrictions.

Can I do video calls and meetings?

Yes. That is exactly what the setup is designed for. Video calls, cloud-based work and normal remote-work applications should work without a problem. As with any internet connection, an absolute 100% guarantee is not possible.

When do we work?

The preferred work window is in the morning and late morning. This makes the day easier to plan for the whole crew and leaves more afternoons available for sailing or changing anchorage. You can of course continue working later, but you should not expect the entire boat to remain silent around you.

Will there be proper workspaces aboard?

Yes. We plan to create dedicated or well-suited work areas aboard. The exact setup depends on the final boat and fit-out. The goal is to make laptop work, video calls and focused work realistic for several people at the same time.

What happens in bad weather or if connectivity fails?

In bad weather we stay in a protected location and adapt the route to safe conditions. We do not expect major connectivity issues with Starlink, but exceptional weather or technical problems can still cause temporary limitations.

Do we sail every afternoon?

No. Sometimes the best workspace is a quiet anchorage. If work, weather or crew make it sensible, we stay longer. The Workation needs to work first – it is not about maximising nautical miles.

Is the Workation suitable for employees?

Yes, provided your employer explicitly allows you to work from the relevant country during that period. Any necessary approval from your employer is your responsibility.

What is included in the price?

Your berth or booked cabin, the skippers, bed linen and towels are included. The boat and boat insurance are included as well. Starlink is charged separately depending on region, tariff and actual usage.

What is paid from the onboard kitty?

Food, diesel, gas, marina or mooring fees and other shared running costs are paid from the onboard kitty. Personal spending and individual travel insurance are not included.

Can I book on my own?

Yes. Booking one berth in a double cabin is the standard option. For an additional charge, you can book a full cabin for yourself. Two people booking together automatically share one cabin.

What is life aboard like?

Think of it like a good shared flat: we live together, cook together and share responsibility for the boat. This is not an all-inclusive charter. Shore time and personal activities are of course up to you.

Do I have to help with sailing?

No previous experience is required, but an interest in sailing is very welcome. When both skippers are aboard, we can operate the boat without guest assistance if necessary. Still, taking part is part of the concept, and many guests actively want to learn.

How do you handle alcohol aboard?

This is not a party trip. During the week, the work rhythm comes first, so alcohol is more likely to be part of weekends or free evenings. Responsible consumption is generally fine.

Where do we embark and disembark?

We do not plan around one fixed home port and prefer to anchor. Depending on the leg, the crew may therefore be picked up by dinghy from a beach or another suitable meeting point. Exact locations will be shared before each trip.

Is there a minimum age?

Yes. Guests must be at least 18 years old.

Is smoking allowed aboard?

Smoking is allowed only in designated outdoor areas. Smoking inside cabins or interior spaces is not permitted.

Can I bring a pet?

No. We cannot accommodate pets aboard.

Do I need my own travel insurance?

Yes. Each guest is responsible for arranging suitable travel insurance, international health insurance and, if desired, trip cancellation cover.

What if I want to switch off completely?

Then Sailing Sabbatical is probably the better fit: less work structure, longer passages and a more intentional break.
